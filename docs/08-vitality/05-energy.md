## The Mitochondrial View

Most chronic disease reads better as **energy resistance** — energy that no longer flows smoothly — than as an isolated molecular defect.

- Mitochondria are both engine and messenger: they strip electrons from food toward oxygen to make ATP, and they signal cell-to-cell like a distributed inner brain. Their smooth flow *is* your energy.
- "More energy" means smoother flow, not more fuel. You cannot eat your way to more energy, only become more efficient — which is why overeating today lowers today's performance.
- Exercise works through recovery, not the workout. Training briefly raises energy resistance; the body adapts by building more mitochondria, so energy later flows more efficiently.
- It is the stress *response*, not the stressor, that drains you. The chain is mental: an event → the story you tell about it → the stress response → energy cost. Awareness breaks the chain. Acute stress is useful; chronic stress does the damage — see [Recovery → Stress](03-recovery.md#stress).
- Insulin resistance and visceral fat are partly adaptations to sustained excess glucose rather than simple failures (see [Nutrition](04-nutrition.md)).
- Purpose mobilizes the body: a clear aim recruits effort that a diffuse one does not.
- Fatigue is not always laziness. In long-COVID and ME/CFS, muscle shows genuinely lower mitochondrial capacity, and blanket "just exercise" advice can trigger post-exertional crashes. Match load to actual capacity.

## Fitness

- Move every day. Something is far better than nothing.
- The weekly dose: 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity or 75–150 of vigorous, plus muscle strengthening on two or more days. From 65, add balance and strength work on three days a week to prevent falls.[^1]
- Training lowers resting blood pressure, whether it is endurance, resistance or isometric work.[^3]
- Exercise sharpens thinking at every age. Across 133 systematic reviews, even light exercise improved general cognition, memory and executive function.[^4]
- If you sweat, breathe hard, and feel your heart — it's working.
- Morning routine: water → exercise/dance → cold shower.
- Build the basics: carrying, squatting, pulling, and controlled spinal movement. Load advanced variations only under instruction.
- Exercise eases knee osteoarthritis only modestly: about 13 points less pain and 12 points better function on 100-point scales, a gain that may be too small to notice.[^7]
- Stand tall, stretch, develop coordination.
- Routinely lift heavy things. Avoid sitting for long stretches. Adults lose about 0.4% of their muscle mass a year, faster after 75, and strength falls two to five times faster than mass.[^2]

## Depression

*(Mliava)*

Emotional emptiness that eliminates motivation and blocks all directed activity.

Chronic low energy and low mood are often the same state, which is why the first root cause below is physical.

Root causes:

- Insufficient physical or psychic energy.
- No goal worth spending energy on.
- No clear understanding of the situation, preventing a decision.
- Fear of being incapable of achieving a result.
- No step-by-step plan broken into small, rewarded tasks.

*What helps*: address the specific root cause. Restore energy (sleep, movement, nutrition). Clarify the goal — see [Vision](../05-mastery/03-vision.md). Break it into the smallest possible next step — see [Discipline](../05-mastery/04-discipline.md). Act — even poorly.

*NOTE*: this section is about ordinary low mood and its physical contributors. Clinical depression is a medical condition — persistent low mood, loss of interest, or thoughts of self-harm warrant a clinician, not a self-management plan.

## Burnout

| Term | Definition | Formula | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Burnout** | A syndrome of chronic work stress that was not managed: exhaustion, mental distance or cynicism toward the job, and reduced efficacy | chronic work stress − recovery | An occupational phenomenon, not a medical condition; it applies to work only[^5] |

- Burnout grows from a mismatch between person and job in six areas: workload, control, reward, community, fairness and values.[^6]
