## Desire

| Term | Definition | Formula | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Spontaneous desire** | Wanting sex before any touch | desire → arousal | Usual early in a relationship |
| **Responsive desire** | Wanting sex that appears only after touch has started arousal | arousal → desire | Usual in long relationships, more common in women; not a lack of desire[^1] |

## Body

- The clitoris is mostly internal. The visible glans is its tip; its two legs and two bulbs lie inside, around the vaginal opening, and their size varies widely between women.[^2]
- Most women do not reach orgasm from intercourse alone. In a US national sample, 18% said penetration alone was enough; most need or enjoy direct clitoral touch as well.[^3]
- Decades of imaging, dissection and lab work have not found a distinct "G-spot".[^10]
- Squirting is mostly diluted urine from the bladder. Female ejaculation proper is a small amount of whitish fluid from the glands beside the urethra.[^9]
- After menopause, falling estrogen leaves the vaginal tissue thin and dry, and sex can hurt. Lubricants, moisturizers and low-dose vaginal estrogen treat it; it is often not diagnosed.[^4]
- Erectile dysfunction is an early warning of heart disease. Men with it have about 45% more cardiovascular events, apart from the usual risk factors.[^8]
- Numbness after vibrator use is usually brief and mostly gone within an hour. A lasting loss of feeling from vibrators is not an established condition.[^6]

## Practice

- Talk about sex. Couples who say what they like report better sexual function and more sexual and relationship satisfaction.[^5]
- Premature ejaculation is treatable. Anaesthetic creams or sprays and medication lengthen the time; the stop–start technique helps while it is practised.[^12]
- "Vaginal rejuvenation" by laser or radio-frequency is not approved for that use and can cause burns, scarring and pain during sex.[^11]
- A young child touching its genitals is normal. Teach that it is private, not that it is bad: shame breeds guilt and secrecy. See a child specialist when it happens many times every day, goes on in public after the talk, or comes with withdrawal or sexual talk.[^7]
