## The Stance

- Health is a tool, not a goal. Obsessing over health is its own disease.
- The older you get, the more effort health requires.
- Fear does not change behavior. [Discipline](../05-mastery/04-discipline.md) does.
- Know your own limits — they differ from others'.
- Use only what you need. Avoid excess.
- Medicine treats what has developed; habit shapes what develops.
- Detoxification is done by the liver and kidneys, not by cleanses, single foods, or sweating. Colon cleansing has no proven benefit; case reports describe salt imbalance, blood infection, a torn bowel and death.[^11]

The mindset this rests on — self-acceptance, purpose, curiosity, dropping blame — is in [Mindset](../05-mastery/02-mindset.md) and [Rajdo](../04-human/23-rajdo.md).

## Habits

- Get regular checkups. Catch problems early.
- Dental checkups at the interval your dentist advises.
- Get a full dilated eye exam by 40. Glaucoma takes side vision with no early symptoms, and a family history raises the risk four to nine times.[^7]
- At a screen you blink up to half as often, so the eyes dry and strain. Every 20 minutes look about 6 m away for 20 seconds; long screen time does no permanent damage.[^8]
- Colorectal screening now starts at 45 in many guidelines, earlier when a close relative had colorectal cancer, and at any age when symptoms appear. Sedation makes endoscopy painless.
- Gallstones run in families and are more common in women: estrogen raises the cholesterol in bile, and progesterone slows the gallbladder's emptying. Most stones cause no symptoms; an ultrasound finds them.
- Health requires daily effort. Small habits, done consistently, compound; panic and "magic pills" do not.
- Mortality is lowest at a body-mass index of about 20–25 and rises both below and above it.[^5]
- Daily habit influences many chronic conditions, though genetics, infection, environment and occupation decide others.

## Warning Signs

- A recurring symptom is a signal, not a normal state. "Everyone has it" is not a standard — compare yourself with your own healthy baseline.
- Duration is the risk. Heartburn, bloating, or indigestion endured for years lets chronic inflammation progress; most digestive cancers are found late because the signs were tolerated, not because there were none.
- A stool calprotectin test helps tell gut inflammation, such as Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis, from irritable bowel, and spares many people an endoscopy.[^12]
- A symptom that fades is not always healed. Long irritation can dull the nerves while the damage goes on — Barrett's esophagus often comes without typical heartburn.
- Stool is the daily feedback on digestion. A one-off change means little; a change that repeats and grows more frequent is a reason to see a doctor. A daily movement is not required: fewer than three a week counts as constipation.[^6]
- A food that newly causes trouble is a symptom to check, not a food to cut. Dropping whole food groups narrows the diet, and a self-labelled intolerance can hide inflammation underneath. IgG food-intolerance panels measure exposure, not intolerance — a diet built on them cuts foods without cause.
- Celiac disease can start in adulthood. A standard blood test checks for it, taken while gluten is still in the diet. Without a diagnosis, cutting gluten brings no proven benefit.
- Lasting tiredness is a symptom, not a mood. Common causes a doctor can check are short sleep, sleep apnea, depression, anemia, thyroid disease, diabetes, low vitamin B12 and medication side effects. The history chooses the tests: blood tests without a specific reason rarely change anything.[^2]
- Squeezing or tight chest pain, especially spreading to the arm, neck or jaw, is a reason to call an ambulance at once. Do not drive yourself.[^10]
- With diarrhea or vomiting, drink in small sips; an oral rehydration solution also replaces lost salts. Bloody diarrhea is a reason to call for medical advice; vomit with blood, or that looks like coffee grounds, calls for an ambulance.[^13] Do not take loperamide for bloody diarrhea or diarrhea with fever: slowing the gut can then cause a dangerously swollen colon.[^14]
- Flashes of light, many new floaters at once, or a grey curtain over part of the view call for an eye doctor the same day: the retina may be detaching.[^9]
- Ask for the cause, not for comfort. Chronic gastritis, for example, is often driven by *H. pylori*, which treatment clears. Treat it even without symptoms: clearing it in healthy carriers cuts later stomach cancer by about half.[^15]

## The Body

1. Consistency beats intensity: what you do daily decides more than what you do hard.
2. Sleep is the habit the others depend on.
3. Movement is not optional, and something beats nothing.
4. Eat food you would recognize as food.
5. Recovery is when the adaptation happens, not the session.

## Hygiene

- Keep your body and hair clean. Wash hands before eating and after the bathroom. Plain soap is enough; antibacterial soap adds nothing in daily use.
- Water pick, floss, brush, and tongue scrape morning and night. Use toothpaste with at least 1,000 ppm fluoride: it prevents more decay than toothpaste without.[^4]
- Protect your hearing.
- Design and keep your own style in clothes.

## Household Environment

- Shoes off at the door.
- Avoid plastic where you can.
- Circulate air in your rooms.
- Fine particles (PM2.5) are the air pollutant that harms most, mainly through heart disease and stroke. WHO's guideline is an annual mean of 5 µg/m³.[^3]
- Avoid long-distance travel where you can.

## Connection and Focus

- See at least one friend a week. Strong social ties raise the odds of survival by about half, an effect on the scale of smoking and alcohol.[^1]
- Turn notifications off by default and grant them back by name.
- Limit social media use.
- Never text while driving.
