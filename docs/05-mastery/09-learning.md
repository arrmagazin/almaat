`Learning` is defined formally in [Intellect](../03-mind/11-intellect.md) as the update of `Memory`, and thereby of `Knowledge` and `Skill`, that makes them better predict further `Experience`. This file is the applied side: how to drive that update on purpose.

## The Learning Loop

A `Mind` improves by closing a loop, not by absorbing `Information`:

1. **Act** — produce a `Prediction` or attempt a `Skill`.
2. **Feedback** — compare the result against `Experience`, not against opinion.
3. **Validate** — keep what predicted well, revise what didn't. This is `Validation` driven by `Doubt`.
4. **Update** — let the correction reshape `Memory`, so the next attempt starts higher.

Without feedback there is no learning, only repetition.

## Deliberate Practice

Reps alone plateau. [`Practice`](01-mastery.md#terms) compounds only when aimed at the edge of what you cannot yet do.

**Aim just past your reach.** Too easy means no growth; too hard means noise. Seek the band where you fail often enough to correct and rarely enough to keep going.

**Discomfort is the signal.** Comfort means you have left the band where correction happens.

- **Aim at the weakness.** Drill the specific sub-skill that fails, not the whole performance you already pass.
- **Tighten the feedback.** Shorten the gap between attempt and correction; delayed feedback teaches little (see [Manipulation → Conditioning](../07-sustain/09-manipulation.md#conditioning)).
- **Volume over perfection.** Many corrected attempts beat a few flawless ones; the reps are what reshape `Memory`. Staying in that discomfort is what [Discipline](04-discipline.md) is for, and years of it is [`Mastery`](01-mastery.md).

## Making It Stick

Taking information in is not retaining it. Recognizing a page is not understanding it: rereading and highlighting make the material familiar, and familiarity feels like understanding until the source is gone. Knowing the [`Name`](../03-mind/05-knowledge.md#naming) of a thing is not knowing the thing. Retention has its own mechanics:

> I learned very early the difference between knowing the name of something and knowing something. — Richard Feynman

| Move | Why it works |
| ---- | ------------ |
| **Rebuild over copy** | Read once, close the source, rebuild it from nothing, then compare. Each difference marks a gap; the failed attempts are the learning, not a setback. Copied work leaves you dependent on the source — able to follow the tutorial, unable to go on when it ends |
| **Retrieval over review** | Recalling from memory (self-testing) builds durable `Memory`; rereading only feels productive |
| **Spacing over cramming** | Revisiting across days forces re-retrieval and strengthens the trace; massed study fades fast |
| **Interleaving** | Mixing problem types trains you to *choose* the method, not just execute a known one |
| **Teaching it** | Explaining in plain words exposes the gaps a `Bias` hides — if you cannot teach it, you have not learned it. Explain it to someone outside the field: every term you cannot drop and every sentence you must rewrite marks a gap. Jargon hides gaps; plain words show them |
| **Writing by hand** | Handwriting is slower and recruits motor planning alongside language, so the note is encoded while it is being made. Typing into a note app captures the text without encoding it — the archive grows and the `Memory` does not |
| **Rest inside practice** | Brief pauses between attempts are when the gain is written. Early in practice most of the gain appears in rests of a few seconds, while the brain replays the sequence about 20 times faster than it was done. Replaying it deliberately, the rep imagined done cleanly, strengthens the same trace[^4] |
| **Sleep** | Consolidation happens during rest, not at the desk; see [Recovery → Sleep](../08-vitality/03-recovery.md#sleep). Sleep soon after study: what is learned within a few hours of sleep is kept better than what is followed by a day awake[^3] |

## Reading

Reading is the highest-bandwidth way into other `Minds` — compressed `Experience` you did not have to live. It pays only when active: question the text, connect it to what you already hold, and convert it into an attempt. A book read without application is `Information` that never became `Knowledge`.

Keep a short list of open problems you cannot stop thinking about, and test new input against it: which problem does this serve? Keep what serves one and link it to that problem at once; drop the rest without regret. A fact that arrives with a question to answer has a place in `Memory` and stays; a fact with no place fades.

## Languages

- Learn a language in chunks: word groups that native speakers use as one unit, such as *at the time* or *having said that*. A larger stock of chunks goes with more fluent speech.[^1]
- Learn the most frequent words first. About 2,000–3,000 word families cover 95% of everyday speech, enough to follow much of it; reading without help takes about 8,000–9,000.[^2]
- Distance from your own language sets the time. For English speakers in full-time study, the US Defense Language Institute teaches French or Spanish in 36 weeks, Russian or Persian in 48, and Arabic, Chinese, Japanese or Korean in 64.[^5]
- Age limits the ceiling, not the start. The ability to learn a new grammar holds until about 17 and then declines, and a native-like command is rare unless learning began by about 10.[^6]
- Watch with captions in the language you are learning. They improve listening comprehension and vocabulary over watching without them.[^7]
