## Determination

Determination *(Nastyrnost)*
: an attitude toward the process rather than the outcome, which allows a decision to be made without waiting for perfect conditions.

*NOTE*: requires control over the [`Animal`](../04-human/28-animal.md) and over the [`Dark Shadow`](../06-socium/34-identity.md).

---

Vision *(Bachanne\*)*
: the picture of the desired future, in the world and in oneself, that the `Mission` is pursued toward.
: Answers *what does it look like when the `Mission` is being fulfilled?* Reachable; revised when reached.

---

Define your authentic Way — life's mission, ambitions, high-level goals.
**Authentic** is the load-bearing word. A `Goal` copied from a peer, a parent, or an approved norm has no [`Core-Value`](../03-mind/09-teleos.md) behind it, so `Will` finds nothing to hold when `Circumstances` shift — which is why a goal set as an obligation rarely survives a change of `Circumstances`. The test is whether the goal produces a felt response rather than an approving one: a `Goal` you cannot feel belongs to someone else.

> Magnum opus viros magnificos facit. — A great work makes great men.

*Define your lifelong aims, drilled down into small concrete everyday goals, which will wake you up every morning and thrill you all along the time.*

Make the goals specific and hard. Across decades of studies, a specific, difficult goal led to higher performance than an easy one or a plain "do your best".[^1]

*The Conflict*: Determination creates an existential tension between immediate animal satisfaction and long-term rational goals. This confrontation defines the human being's tragedy and greatness. [Self-Honesty](02-mindset.md#self-honesty) converts that tension from a battle into fuel.

## Build Mental Models

Create mental models of your circumstances, processes, goals, and obstacles. Refine them through experience until they become simple and useful.

- Understand the system you're operating in — its rules, dynamics, and leverage points.
- Identify the critical path — the few actions that create the most impact.
- Anticipate obstacles and plan how to overcome them.
- Plan backward: picture the goal done, then trace the steps back to today. Against forward planning, it raised motivation and performance and gave more realistic time estimates.[^2]

## Seasons

Break the year into 90-day seasons. Long enough for real progress, short enough to see the finish line.

Every quarter:

- Reflect on what worked.
- Reset what didn't.
- Add one thing to your **To-Don't List** (subtraction > addition).

## Strategic Reviews

**Regret Review**: Identify last year's biggest regret. Extract the lesson. Create a plan to prevent repetition.

**Pre-mortem**: Imagine December 31st — your goal failed. Why? Design the year to block those failure modes.

**Dependency Check**: If your income, your business, or your main support disappeared tomorrow, do you know your next move? Each "no" marks a dependence on stability. Build the fallback while things are calm — a crisis rehearsed in advance arrives as a working situation, not a surprise.

> Try to do everything properly — failure will happen on its own. — Mikolaj Filip Ryzhko
>
> Life is unpredictable, problems unavoidable, but persist toward your goals regardless — because you decided so.

## Questions on Paper

A problem exists only as long as it stays blurry. Written down concretely, most of it dissolves into a task with a solution.

Thinking in the head is fast but skips steps, hides assumptions, and agrees with itself. On paper every step is visible, so a jump, a hidden assumption, or a circular argument can be found and fixed. Write the whole chain of reasoning, not only the conclusion; for a decision, write the full case for each option.

| # | Question | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What exactly is the problem?** | Blurriness is the problem's main defense. Stated concretely, much of it resolves into a task rather than a catastrophe | Not "I have no time," but "I spend 3 hours a day on social media instead of working." |
| 2 | **What am I doing right now that makes it worse?** | People get stuck not from not knowing the way out, but from continuing what creates the problem. Remove the action against yourself and it starts to solve itself | Wanting a business, but spending every weekend drinking beer |
| 3 | **What would I do if I weren't afraid?** | Blows up excuses. You always know the answer; fear of losing comfort blocks it. Written down honestly, the brain stops treating it as a threat and starts looking for ways to implement it | Naming the feared outcome, rather than the excuse for avoiding it |
| 4 | **What price am I paying for inaction?** | Use numbers — money, years, missed chances. Once the real price is visible, fear of change becomes smaller than fear of staying | Postponing a job change: the years of pay difference it costs |
| 5 | **What will I do right now?** | Not tomorrow — within the hour. One action. A decision without immediate action turns into a daydream | Wrote the resume. Made the call. Deleted the app |

When direction is present but the energy to move on it is not, the block is usually physical — see [Energy → Depression](../08-vitality/05-energy.md#depression).
