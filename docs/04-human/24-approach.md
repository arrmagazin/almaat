# Approaching Rajdo

It tends to surface only *after* long effort, and then only when the effort is released — never by force. Practices do not manufacture it; they clear what obscures it.

## The Shift

- **Observe rather than hold on.** The first move is to notice that `Thought`s, feelings, and the body are *observed* — they pass through; they are not the observer. This is [`Awareness`](23-rajdo.md#what-rajdo-entails).
- **Peaks are not it.** Altered and peak states — bliss, dissolution, oneness — come and go. `Rajdo` is not a high but the even, stable ground beneath highs and lows. Equanimity, not intensity.
- **The inner critic falls quiet.** What the shift removes is the compulsive, nagging voice of unworthiness — *you are doing the wrong thing, you must achieve, you are defective*. That voice is the interiorized crowd, not you (see [inner speech](../2-mind/12-mind.md#the-social-origin-of-mind)); `Rajdo` is dis-identification from it.

## Traps on the Way

- **The state that evaporates.** A peak reached at a retreat dissolves on return to ordinary life; treating the peak as the destination sends you chasing the next one forever.
- **The post-peak crash.** Losing a vivid state can drop a person into despair. The state was a glimpse, not a possession; the ground it pointed to does not leave.
- **Fanaticism.** Taking a call to give up everything literally, or surrendering judgment to a teacher who accepts only the fearless, turns a path of `Moderation` into self-harm. Fearlessness without discernment is exploitable.

## The Mirror and the Two Intentions

A yogic reading (Patanjali's *Yoga Sutras*, via *Autobiography of a Yogi*) gives `Rajdo` a practical mechanism.

**The outer mirrors the inner.** Circumstances tend to return the content of one's inner state — *karma* read as feedback, not punishment.

Changing only the circumstances — new job, city, relationship — reproduces the same pattern, because what generates it is unchanged. The work is therefore upstream, at the self: loosening the identification between the constructed self and the awareness it runs on. This is the same move as [`Self-Acceptance`](23-rajdo.md#what-rajdo-entails) — stop identifying with the pattern, and the pattern loosens.

**Force and surrender.** Two ways to pursue an aim:

- *Personal will* — self-generated force: "I want this, I will make it happen." It works up to the limit of one's own energy, then plateaus into burnout and a recurring ceiling; the only tool left is more of the force that already stalled.
- *Surrender* — `Acceptance` applied to outcomes: act with full commitment, then release the fruit of the action. The `Agent` stops being the sole source of the effort. Holding on to the result — checking, measuring the gap, monitoring — is itself the obstruction; the result tends to arrive once the monitoring stops. This is not passivity: effort is total, only attachment to the specific form is dropped. See `Moderation` and `Gratitude`.

## Practices

- **Breath inward.** A daily seated breathing practice that draws attention inward and steadies the mind before any external input (see [Breathing](../9-vitality/03-recovery.md#breathing)).
- **The mirror journal.** Take one stuck area and ask, "if this is a mirror, what is it reflecting?" — follow it down to the belief that carries a charge. A belief you can state plainly can be worked with; one that stays unexamined keeps operating unchanged.
- **Chosen state in company.** Hold a genuine inner state of calm and goodwill in a public setting and watch how interactions shift — direct evidence that inner state is not private.
- **Weekly intention.** Clarify a *direction* that is genuinely yours (not a specific form), commit fully, then explicitly release the timeline and the shape it takes.
